Overview

Trustworthiness beyond the model boundary

This workshop brings together researchers in trustworthy machine learning, AI safety, system security, and governance to address risks that emerge when foundation AI models are deployed as autonomous agents in complex, high-stakes environments.

System-level focus

Agentic AI systems interact with external tools, access local resources, and take partial control over system operations. Trustworthiness must account for interactions among models, tools, infrastructure, human oversight, and deployment context together.

Interdisciplinary community

The workshop aims to connect computer science, software engineering, cybersecurity, trustworthy machine learning, AI safety, and governance communities around system-level frameworks for reliable agentic systems.

Asia-Pacific opportunity

With NeurIPS 2026 being held on-site in Australia for the first time, the workshop will amplify diverse voices from the Asia-Pacific region while convening global academic, industry, government, and public-sector stakeholders.

Topics

Research themes

We welcome original contributions on LLM trustworthiness, agentic AI autonomy, agentic safety, agentic reliability, governance, and broader paths toward reliable AI systems.

LLM Trustworthiness

Multi-dimensional evaluation and improvement of LLMs across safety, fairness, robustness, privacy, and ethics for reliable deployment.

Agentic AI Autonomy

Architectural design, planning, memory, and tool-use capabilities of LLM-based autonomous agents that operate with extended autonomy.

Adversarial Attacks

Prompt injection, jailbreaks, malicious external instructions, and defenses that protect aligned agent behavior.

Multi-Agent Coordination

Cascading failures, rogue agent infiltration, covert inter-agent collusion, and defenses for multi-agent systems.

Agent-Tool Integration

Trust boundary violations and privilege escalation when agents interact with external tools or protocols such as MCP.

Agent-Memory Attacks

Attacks that compromise long-term memory or knowledge bases of LLM agents, along with defenses against them.

Agentic Knowledge Attacks

Knowledge base poisoning, backdoored retrievers, and corrupted upstream components that persistently manipulate agents.

Control Flow Attacks

Runtime attacks that hijack agent decision-making by manipulating planning, and defenses against them.

Privacy & Data Leakage

Training data extraction, membership inference, sensitive data leakage, and mitigation strategies.

Hallucination & Factuality

Factual errors compounding across multi-step reasoning chains, plus evaluation and mitigation for factual reliability.

Human Oversight

Meaningful human intervention mechanisms that address scalable oversight and automation-bias risks.

Interpretability

Understanding LLM internals to support auditing, debugging, and behavioral intervention in deployed agent systems.

Testing & Evaluation

Dynamic testing, fault localization, and systematic evaluation of agentic systems under real deployment conditions.

Governance

Regulatory frameworks and institutional practices for responsibility, auditability, and compliance in high-stakes domains.

Broader Path to Reliable AI

Architectural and training advances beyond next-token prediction toward richer world models and reliable internal representations.

Call for Papers

Submissions and participation

We plan to solicit short research papers and extended abstracts on trustworthy, secure, reliable, and governable agentic AI systems. Submission dates, OpenReview link, and camera-ready instructions will be announced after acceptance.

Diversity & Inclusion

Broad participation by design

Trustworthy agentic AI spans technical, societal, governance, and industrial dimensions. The workshop will intentionally include organizers and speakers from multiple continents, with representation across genders, disciplines, and sectors.

MentoringEach accepted paper will be paired with an experienced researcher for pre-workshop feedback, supporting first-time presenters and authors from less-resourced institutions.
Financial supportTravel grants and registration waivers for authors from underrepresented groups will be explored through industrial sponsorship.
Inclusive CFPThe call for papers will use inclusive language encouraging submissions from researchers of all backgrounds and career stages.
Targeted outreachWith NeurIPS 2026 in Australia, outreach will target the Asia-Pacific community and voices underrepresented at top-tier machine learning venues.
